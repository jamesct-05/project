Water Skiing

Water skiing is a sport where a person glides across the water on one or two skis while being pulled by a boat. The skier holds onto a tow rope attached to the boat and uses balance and body position to stay upright as the boat moves.

A skier usually starts in the water with their knees bent and skis pointing forward. As the boat pulls, the skier rises gradually and keeps their weight balanced over the skis. Beginners often start with two skis, while more experienced skiers may use a single ski.

Water skiing also includes several competition events, including slalom, tricks, and jumping. Slalom skiers weave around a course of buoys, trick skiers perform maneuvers, and jumpers use a ramp to travel as far as possible. Wearing a life jacket and following safe boating practices are important whenever people ski behind a boat.



Jumping over the boat's wake is one trick a water skier can try. The wake acts like a small ramp, helping the skier lift briefly above the water. A skier should first be comfortable balancing and turning before attempting a jump.

After leaving the water, the skier should stay balanced and prepare to land with knees bent to absorb the impact. It is important to start with small, controlled jumps in a clear area and only with a capable boat driver watching. Beginners should learn from an experienced instructor and always wear a properly fitted life jacket.
