Wakeboarding

Wakeboarding is a water sport where a rider stands on a board and is towed behind a boat by a rope. Riders can cruise across the water or use the boat's wake to practice turns and, as their skills improve, jumps.

For a first ride, the guide recommends getting comfortable with your bindings and stance before entering the water. Start floating in a tucked position with your knees bent and arms straight; when the boat pulls gradually, stay low until the board moves beneath you, then rise while keeping your knees bent.

To steer, shift your weight onto the heel-side or toe-side edge of the board rather than twisting the board sharply. Make smooth, gradual movements as you practice riding and crossing the wake.

Take your time learning the basics before trying jumps or advanced tricks. Falls are part of learning, so practice with appropriate supervision and safety gear.

Wakeboarding is my second favorite water activity after tubing. I love to go back and forth, on and off the wake. I have recently been trying to learn both jumps and switches.

Jumping the wake is a decent challenge for me, since I struggle to build up speed. And switching the board so I have my right foot forward is a big struggle, as I find myself always drifting to one side, but hopefully next summer I will figure it out.
