Tubing

Water tubing is my personal favorite of the four sports listed on this site. It is a fun activity for many ages and can be a great introduction to other watersports. Towable tubes come in different sizes and styles, so choose one that suits the riders who will use it.

Before heading out, make sure you have a tube, a suitable tow rope, and properly fitting life jackets. Assign one person to drive the boat and another to watch the riders. Attach the rope securely, and review hand signals so riders and the spotter can communicate.

Choose a tube that matches the riders' ages, sizes, and comfort levels. A properly fitted life jacket is especially important because riders may fall into the water. Brightly colored jackets can also make it easier for the spotter to see riders.

Boat drivers should keep watch in every direction for other boats, docks, shorelines, trees, and other obstacles. A tube can swing wide in the opposite direction of a turn, so the driver should leave plenty of space and avoid sharp turns. Smooth, gradual changes in speed can also help reduce sudden pulls on the tow rope.

The spotter should keep their attention on the riders and use the agreed hand signals to communicate with the driver. Riders should hold on securely and signal if they need the boat to stop. After someone falls, the driver and spotter should make sure the area is clear and check that the rider is okay before continuing.
